# ADR-011 — Account and Person Identity Model

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2026-09-11  
**Owners:** Accounts, Identity, and Trayvi architecture

## Context

Trayvi needs one durable relationship for a person that can acquire several future capabilities
without copying identity data or creating a base account per role. Phase 1 must reserve duplicate
documents safely under retries and concurrency, but authentication, permissions, role attachment,
recovery, and provider selection belong to later phases.

## Decision drivers

Single-person cardinality, privacy, database-authoritative concurrency, explicit trust evidence,
future role extensibility, safe duplicate behavior, lifecycle accountability, and compatibility with
the modular-monolith foundation.

## Considered options

- A single `users` table containing credentials, roles, document data, and profile state: rejected
  because it conflates independent lifecycles and encourages sensitive-data access.
- One account per role: rejected because it violates the single base relationship and duplicates
  identity/recovery concerns.
- Separate Account and Person Identity aggregates composed by one local transaction: selected.

## Decision

Accounts owns the base `Account`, `EnrollmentOperation`, and account transition history. Identity
owns `PersonIdentity`, `IdentityDocument`, `FingerprintClaim`, `VerificationCase`,
`VerificationEvidence`, `Review`, and identity transition history. The modules expose inward-facing
domain/application contracts; neither imports the other's infrastructure. `apps/api` is the current
composition root and adds no public Phase 1 endpoint.

Every person identity references exactly one account, and `person_identities.account_id` is unique.
An account created by enrollment has exactly one identity in committed state. Future roles attach to
the opaque account ID; Phase 1 creates no role rows or role behavior. All entity and evidence IDs are
opaque UUIDv7 values generated by the Foundation primitive.

Account states are `PENDING_IDENTITY`, `ACTIVE`, `RESTRICTED`, `SUSPENDED`, and terminal `CLOSED`.
Identity states are `PENDING`, `ESTABLISHED`, `RESTRICTED`, and terminal `RETIRED`. Verification,
review, document, and claim lifecycles remain separate. Material account/identity transitions require
the expected aggregate version, actor class/reference, safe reason code, evidence reference, and
timestamp; the same transaction appends the transition and safe outbox events. PostgreSQL triggers
reject illegal edges, stale/shape-invalid updates, missing history, activation without an established
identity and bound claim, and mutation of append-only evidence.

`StartAccountCreation` writes only an expiring, keyed-idempotent operation. It does not create an
account. `SubmitIdentityInformation` prepares protected material and then, on one PostgreSQL
connection and transaction, locks the operation and creates the pending account, pending identity,
document envelope, all lookup-active fingerprint claims, transition history, safe outbox events, and
stored idempotency result. The partial unique fingerprint index selects a single winner. A loser rolls
back all aggregate writes and records only a generic conflict on its pre-existing operation.

New, replayed, racing, and duplicate submissions return the same public accepted result shape and no
account/identity ID or duplicate reason. Support receives no lookup or mutation bypass. A trusted,
provider-neutral decision with immutable evidence may later bind the claim, establish the identity,
and activate the account atomically. Closing/retiring never silently reopens the relationship or
releases its blocking claim.

## Consequences

Cross-module creation deliberately uses one local PostgreSQL unit of work. This preserves atomicity
inside the modular monolith but means future service extraction would require a new consistency ADR.
Duplicate recovery, role assignment, reviewer authorization, and authentication stay unavailable
until their owning phases define proof and permissions.

## Security/privacy/operational impact

The unauthenticated result is not an account-existence oracle. Document material remains inside
Identity, transition evidence is immutable, runtime roles have no DDL/delete privileges, and
database constraints remain authoritative even when application validation is bypassed. Pending
reservation expiry and release are disabled until product, Trust/Safety, and retention policy approve
them.

## Validation and revisit triggers

Validate with exhaustive state matrices, direct-SQL denial tests, atomic failure injection, replay
tests, and ten real-PostgreSQL rounds of fifty simultaneous same-document submissions. Revisit only
for an approved recovery/reactivation policy, a new person-linking assurance model, measured service
extraction, or a role requirement that cannot attach to the existing account.
